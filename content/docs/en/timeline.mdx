# The Timeline

<Callout variant="info">
Your memory is not a folder of chat threads. It is a single continuous line. Every dot is a conversation. Click any dot and you're there — not searching, not scrolling through a sidebar, but traveling to a specific moment in time.
</Callout>

The timeline is Previously's core navigation model — a horizontal, scrollable line where every conversation becomes a visible point. It replaces the chat-thread list entirely. There is no sidebar full of named conversations. There is no search bar. There is one line, from your earliest memory to right now.

## Not a Chat List, Not a Search Bar

Most AI products give you one of two things: a vertical list of named conversation threads, or a search bar. Both assume you will manage your own memory — name things, delete things, remember what you called something three weeks ago.

Previously rejects that assumption. Your memory is organized by time because time is the one axis you never have to curate. You don't name moments. You experience them, in order. The timeline reflects that.

## How It Looks

The timeline lives at the top of the page, just below the navigation bar. It stays visible as you scroll through the content below — sticky, always in reach.

```
[← Load earlier] ●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──●──○ [NOW]
                  7/20  7/21  7/22  7/23  7/24  7/25  7/26  7/27
                  14:30 09:15 11:00 15:45 10:30 16:00 08:00 13:30
```

Each node on the timeline is three elements stacked vertically:

- **Date** (top) — the day the conversation happened, in short format
- **Dot** (center) — a filled circle for past conversations, a hollow circle for NOW
- **Time** (bottom) — the hour and minute the conversation started

A single thin horizontal line runs through all the dots, connecting them. It says: these moments are not isolated. They belong to one continuous story.

The selected node glows — the dot enlarges slightly with a halo ring around it. Unselected nodes are quieter, semi-transparent gray. The visual hierarchy is clear: one moment is in focus, the rest are accessible but not demanding attention.

Newer conversations are on the right. Older ones are on the left. You read left to right, from past to present — the natural reading direction for most of the world.

## NOW

The rightmost node is different. It is hollow — an empty circle where a filled one should be. Its label is not a date. It says **NOW**.

This is the boundary between the past and the present. Clicking NOW takes you to the live conversation — the active slice, the input bar, the present moment. It is always there, always available, a single click away from any point in your history.

The NOW node is also a visual promise: this timeline is not complete. There is always room for what comes next.

## Click Any Dot — Time Travel

This is where the timeline stops being decoration and becomes interaction.

Click any dot on the timeline. The current view doesn't flash, doesn't go blank, doesn't show a loading spinner. The old content stays exactly where it is. A subtle pulse bar appears at the very top of the content area — a thin line, barely noticeable — to tell you something is loading. When the new content is ready, it replaces the old. No flicker. No whiplash.

This is **stable slice switching**. A `SliceCache` in the browser keeps recently viewed conversations warm for 5 minutes, so clicking back to a dot you just visited is instant.

What loads is the full conversation — every exchange, every reply, exactly as it happened. And next to every agent message, there's a small link that says **Thoughts.** Click it, and a dialog opens showing what the agent was thinking during that exact turn. Not what it *said*. What it was *thinking* while it said it. The tool calls it made. The reasoning it went through. The decisions it considered and rejected.

This is possible because every slice stores two parallel records: `core.md` (the conversation) and `agent.md` (the cognition). The "Thoughts" link reads the cognition record for that specific turn. You are looking at the agent's internal monologue from that moment in the past.

## Scrolling Through Your Past

The timeline is horizontal. Your mouse wheel — which normally scrolls vertically — scrolls the timeline horizontally. This is a deliberate choice: the timeline is not a list you scan up and down. It is a line you travel along, left to right.

On a touch device, you swipe horizontally. On a desktop, you scroll with the wheel or drag with the trackpad.

A "Load earlier" button sits at the far left. Click it to fetch older slices — further back in time. The timeline extends, new dots appear, and the line grows longer.

## What's Behind Each Dot

Every dot on the timeline is backed by a **slice directory** — not just a single file, but a small folder containing everything from that moment:

```
memory/episodic/slices/2026/07/27/1430/
  timeline/
    core.md       ← the conversation (what was said)
    agent.md      ← the cognition (what the agent was thinking)
  previously.md   ← the agent's self-model at that moment
```

The dual timeline (`core.md` + `agent.md`) is a v0.5 innovation. In earlier versions, the agent's thinking was ephemeral — it existed during the conversation and then disappeared. Now it is preserved alongside the conversation, making the "Thoughts" link possible and giving the agent the ability to reflect on its own past reasoning.

The `previously.md` snapshot captures the agent's understanding of you at that exact moment. You can see how its impression of you evolved over time — what it believed after your third conversation versus your thirtieth.

## The Global Timeline Index

Behind the visual timeline is a single flat file: `memory/episodic/timeline.md`. It contains a summary of every closed slice in your history — one entry per conversation. This is the recall system's starting point. When the agent needs to search your past, it reads this one file and immediately knows everything that's available, without scanning directories or opening multiple indexes.

The index is regenerated when new slices close. If it's more than 6 hours old when the agent reads it, it auto-regenerates. You never need to think about it.

## Data Flow

The timeline is populated from your GitHub repository (or local filesystem in development):

1. **Initial load** — the page fetches the global timeline index and renders it as a horizontal strip of dots
2. **Pagination** — clicking "Load earlier" fetches older entries, extending the line leftward
3. **Slice selection** — clicking a dot loads that slice's full content (core.md for the conversation, agent.md for the cognition)
4. **NOW** — returns to the live, active conversation

## The Interaction Model

The timeline is not a decorative element or a sidebar you occasionally glance at. It is the primary navigation surface of the product:

- **Scan** — scroll horizontally to survey your past at a glance
- **Jump** — click any dot to teleport to that moment
- **Reflect** — click "Thoughts" on any historical agent message to see what it was thinking
- **Return** — click NOW to come back to the present

No folders. No naming. No search queries. Just a line through time, and you decide where to stop.
