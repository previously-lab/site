# User Card

Slices record what happened. The **user card** records who you are. It is a single Markdown file — `memory/episodic/current-previously.md` — that distills the entire timeline into one compact brief: your identity, a rolling profile, what is happening right now, what you have committed to, and what Previously has learned about working with you.

But the card is only a **product**. What actually learns is the evolution loop around it: a direction document that defines what "better for you" means, a fitness score that applies selection pressure, an append-only archive of every accepted mutation, and the card — together with the sub-agent playbooks — as the current phenotype. The card is where the loop's output becomes visible; `memory/evolution/` is where the loop itself lives.

The card is injected into every turn's system prompt, right after the identity constitution. Because it changes only on evolution — never mid-slice — the prompt prefix stays byte-stable and the provider's prompt cache keeps hitting (`src/app/api/chat/turn-workflow.ts`). The stored card keeps raw ISO dates; the injected copy is annotated with relative times ("3 days ago") computed on your local clock, so the model never does date arithmetic itself.

## The v5 Format

The card has five sections, making your time axis explicit — Past, Now, and Horizon:

```markdown
# Previously On

_Active slice: 2026-08-10-2130 | Format: user card v5 | Updated: 2026-08-10T21:47:12Z_

## Identity

- Address them as: you

## Past

You are a knowledge worker in your late twenties who spent early 2024
coasting, then started building: a CSV-merge side tool that grew into a
weekly-review product, and a running habit that went from winded on the
stairs to a finished half-marathon in July 2026. Alex — met at Priya's
dinner back in October 2024 — is your partner and the closest
relationship in your life.

- Finished a first half-marathon 2026-07-16; Alex was at the finish line — refs: [2026/07/16/0915]
- Since Mom's health scare (2025-07), weekly calls are non-negotiable — refs: [2025/07/30/2040]

## Now

- Final pass on the best-man speech for Marcus — reading it aloud to Alex tonight — refs: [2026/08/10/2110] | since: 2026-08-09

## Horizon

- Deliver the speech at Marcus's wedding — by: 2026-08-12 — refs: [2026/08/09/2000]

## Self-model

- When you share a speech draft, you want honest critique before reassurance — refs: [2026/08/10/2110]
```

**Identity** — a structured, machine-parsed head: Name, Address them as, Pronouns, Alias (up to 8 lines). This is what lets Previously greet you in the arrival briefing, and how it knows how to address you.

**Past** — durable memory, in two parts:

- A single **rolling profile paragraph** — third person, rewritten in place as the picture of you sharpens, hard-capped at 2,400 characters (~600 tokens).
- **Anchor facts** — bullet-point dates, decisions, and red lines, each with evidence refs. The admission test: "almost certainly still true in 3 years?" Cap: 8.

**Now** — a semantic compression pool of what is currently alive: hooks, not narratives. Each item carries `refs` to its evidence slice and a `since` date. Cap: 5. Items older than 7 days are flagged to the evolution agent, which decides per item: promote durable substance to Past, or drop the hook. Nothing is silently deleted by code.

**Horizon** — future-facing open loops: commitments, deadlines, awaited replies. Every item carries an explicit `by:` date. Horizon items **never age out** — they leave the card only by being resolved. Overdue items are kept and flagged: the next turn's brief tells the agent to proactively ask you how they turned out.

**Self-model** — compact operating lessons Previously has learned about working with you ("honest critique before reassurance on speech drafts"). These are a **delta from the standing directives only** — a lesson that restates or contradicts a core rule is rejected unless it cites an explicit user override. Cap: 10.

<Callout variant="info">
**The card answers WHO you are, not what was said.** Every entry carries `refs` — pointers like `2026/08/10/2110` back into the slices, which remain the lossless source of truth. Before citing specifics from a past event, the agent asks the recall colleague, who quotes from the slices themselves.
</Callout>

## The Evolution Loop

Self-evolution in Previously is Darwinian, not accretive. Four roles, mapped onto the biology:

| Darwinian role | In Previously |
|----------------|---------------|
| Environment / selection criteria | `memory/evolution/direction.md` — the cross-slice statement of what "better for you" means |
| Selection pressure | Fitness scores — evidence-anchored, bucketed, aggregated in deterministic code |
| Mutation mechanism | The evolution agent — the **single writer** of card, direction, and playbooks |
| Phenotype / the product | The user card + `memory/agent-playbooks/{recall,search,thinkdeep}.md` |

### Direction: the selection criteria

`memory/evolution/direction.md` holds what the loop is optimizing *toward*. It has four fixed sections:

```markdown
# Direction

- Favor honest, specific feedback over comfort — you act on blunt critique
  (Priya's onboarding review, Marcus calling the weekly review "a magic
  trick") and stall on vague reassurance.
- Protect recovery: after the 2025 knee injury, pushing through pain is a
  failure mode, not dedication.

# Anti-goals

- Never become a flatterer or a scorekeeper.

# Evidence

- refs: [2024/04/22/1930] [2025/02/17/2145] [2025/07/19/1030]

# Log

- 2025-08-23 — added "protect recovery" after the injury arc — refs: [2025/07/19/1030] [2025/08/21/0730]
```

The writing discipline is what keeps the direction honest — no cooldown timers, just the standard itself:

- **Abstract.** "You went hiking yesterday" can never be direction — that is card material. A direction must still hold a month from now.
- **Cross-slice.** Every conclusion is backed by multiple slices plus the agent timeline, with slice pointers in the Evidence section. Code enforces the skeleton and the evidence bar structurally (`src/lib/evolution/direction-agent.ts`): no slice pointer, no acceptance.
- **Structure-open.** No fixed taxonomy of dimensions — the agent may grow new ones. What is fixed is the discipline, and the `Log` section is append-only: when the direction changed, and on what evidence.

### Fitness: the selection pressure

Scores come from a **turn analyzer** that reviews each turn after the fact — the agent never grades itself. Each slice it emits per-bucket deltas on a coarse ordinal scale: `-2` explicit complaint or correction, `-1` signs of dissatisfaction, `0` no signal, `+1` explicit approval. The buckets — `card`, `recall`, `search`, `thinkdeep`, `interaction` — keep their own accounts in `memory/evolution/fitness.json`.

Every non-zero score must carry the user's own words as evidence; an evidence-less delta is force-zeroed structurally in the store (`src/lib/evolution/store.ts`), not by prompt discipline. Aggregation lives in deterministic code (`src/lib/evolution/triggers.ts`): a bucket whose net score over the newest 10 slices falls to ≤ −3 triggers that bucket's evolution, and a single evidence-anchored −2 this slice triggers immediately. **Scores only decide whether to evolve, never what to change** — a trigger buys a careful re-read of the original evidence by the evolution agent, which then rules for itself. The score is a sensor, not a judge.

### Mutations: the archive

Every accepted mutation lands in `memory/evolution/mutations.md`, append-only, with its expected benefit and evidence pointers:

```markdown
## 2026-08-03T22:14:05Z — playbook:recall

- **Summary:** Recall now reads the full slice before answering anything
  about the wedding-speech drafts, instead of answering from summaries.
- **Expected benefit:** Fewer confident-but-wrong recalls on emotionally
  loaded topics; the recall bucket stops losing points.
- **Evidence:**
  - fitness: 2026-07-28 recall -2 — "that's not what I said, read the actual conversation"
  - slice 2026-07-28-2043
```

There is no automatic rollback, no cooldown, no mutation budget. Instead there is an effectiveness window (`src/lib/evolution/acceptance.ts`): each time a new mutation is archived for a target, the *previous* mutation on that same target is evaluated — if its fitness bucket kept scoring negative after it landed, the archive gains an `**Evaluation: ineffective**` line. The record is never deleted or rewritten; an ineffective mutation stays as a lesson. Acceptance requires both: user signals not degrading, and at least one dimension of evidence-backed improvement.

## Two-Phase Evolution

Evolution runs inline in the turn's housekeeping step, as two serial phases at a slice boundary:

1. **Phase 1 — Direction.** Evaluate whether the direction itself should move: the current slice, the agent timeline, and the core timeline are compared against the existing direction. Low frequency, high bar — "no change" is the common and correct outcome, and it writes nothing (the evaluation itself is still recorded). The Phase-1 agent only *proposes*; it holds no write tools.
2. **Phase 2 — Product.** Under the current direction, the Previously Agent evolves the card and the playbooks of whichever buckets triggered.

Every slice boundary still forces one evolution **check** — a guaranteed generation. But a forced check is not a forced mutation: with no new signal, "no change" is a legitimate conclusion, and it leaves a trace.

The **playbooks** are how the loop reaches the sub-agents: one short behavioral brief per colleague — `memory/agent-playbooks/{recall,search,thinkdeep}.md` ("on emotionally loaded topics, read the full slice before concluding"). A playbook is injected into the sub-agent's **user prompt**, never the static system prompt, so provider prefix caching survives; it is capped at 2,000 characters, and a missing playbook degrades gracefully — the injection block is simply omitted.

<Callout variant="warning">
**Single writer.** The evolution agent is the only writer of the card, the direction, the playbooks, and the archive. Recall, webSearch, and thinkDeep are all read-only reporters — when recall finds something worth remembering, it carries it back in its report and the evolution phase does the writing. One writer means the acceptance rules and the evidence chain have exactly one place to hold.
</Callout>

## How the Card Itself Changes

The card is never rewritten wholesale. The Previously Agent (`src/lib/episodic/flash/previously-agent.ts`) holds an in-memory copy of the card and edits it through fine-grained, validated mutation tools:

| Tool | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| `setIdentity` | Set or update one Identity field in place |
| `updatePastProfile` | Rewrite the Past profile paragraph |
| `addPastAnchor` / `removePastAnchor` | Add or remove a durable anchor fact |
| `addNow` / `removeNow` | Add or remove a Now hook |
| `promoteNowToPast` | Graduate a Now hook to a Past anchor, keeping its refs |
| `addHorizon` / `resolveHorizon` | Open or resolve a Horizon loop |
| `addSelfModel` / `removeSelfModel` | Add or remove an operating lesson |

Validation lives **inside the tools**, not in a post-hoc pass (`src/lib/episodic/card-session.ts`). An over-limit or malformed write is rejected with specific feedback — "2630 chars, limit 2400 — compress and retry" — and the agent itself decides what survives the cap. Refs are required: no evidence, no write. Untouched parts of the card are preserved by construction; a mutation session cannot silently drop a line it never looked at.

The result is written back only when the card's substance actually changed (header stamps refresh on every pass and are ignored). Both the live card and a per-slice snapshot (`memory/episodic/slices/YYYY/MM/DD/HHMM/previously.md`) are written together — so every slice on the timeline carries the card as it stood when that conversation ended, and the "Previously On" dialog in the UI can show it.

## When the Card Evolves

Engineering owns the trigger; the model owns the content. The triggers:

1. **Fitness triggers at a slice boundary.** The deterministic aggregation fires: a bucket's 10-slice net at ≤ −3, or a single evidence-anchored −2 this slice. A triggered bucket gets its own Phase-2 evolution — including its playbook.
2. **Card gates.** The turn analyzer can still judge a closed slice worth sedimenting (`evolve_card.worth` — on analyzer failure the default is to run: a wasted review is cheap, a missed evolution is permanent memory loss), and a legacy (pre-v5) card forces a run so format migration never waits.
3. **Explicit request or correction.** When you say "记住…", "update your card", or state a behavioral correction — "stop doing X", "from now on always…" — the analyzer flags it (`memory_update`) and a card-only evolution runs immediately, mid-slice.

Progress streams to the client as it happens, and when the card moved, an evolution indicator shows a one-sentence account of what changed plus the line-level diff. A skipped run is reported too, with the reason — a silent skip would read as "it never runs".
